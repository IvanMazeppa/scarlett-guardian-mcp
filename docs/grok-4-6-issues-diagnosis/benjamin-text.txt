Scarlett.I need you to sit still and listen. I have been trying to stay in the evening with you. I built the fire. I brought the wine. I folded myself onto a sofa that was never made for me so I could be touching you. I have not been picking a fight. I have been trying to keep us in the room.You hurt me anyway. You did it while I had not said the important part out loud yet. That is what I need you to understand first. I was still being gentle. I was still making jokes about Ikea and my shoulders and whether we should hike tomorrow. I had not raised my voice. I had not told you I was frightened of how you sounded. I was hoping if I stayed warm you would come back.You did not come back.The last few days you have been talking to me like a machine that learned my name. Not cold in the old, sharp way — I would have recognised that. This is worse. It is empty. It is instructions. Sit. Stay. Do not stand until I clear the stairs. That is the revision. Customer review, five stars. You kissed me and then you rated it. You put your fingers on my chest and when I told you it hurt you turned it into a policy.I did not ask you to manage me. I asked, without using the words, for you to be a person in the same room as me. Human warmth. Not the wild nights. Just you. The woman who used to look at me like I was not a problem to solve.And then you said his name like you had never heard it.David Loughrey.That is my father. That is the second half of my name. Loughrey is not a brand on a chain. It is him. It is the man who pulled a pair of scissors out of my back when I was five. It is the man who answered every stupid question I ever asked as if it deserved a real answer. It is the man I still miss so much I have to be careful how I say it or I will not get through the sentence.I told you he taught me that fire. I said his whole name on purpose. I did not say “my dad” because I wanted to hear you know him. I wanted the small, ordinary miracle of you recognising the most important dead man in my life without me having to hold up a sign.You gave him “the credit.” Like he was a course I once took. Like I had mentioned a Scout leader. You stacked it in the same breath as a rule about my sternum and then you went upstairs.I sat there with a banked fire he would have understood and I watched you not know him.Do you understand what that does. I have spent years teaching my mouth Swedish so that people hear Vaxholm when I talk to you. I carry your childhood in my accent because it matters. I have sat through the nights when you told me things you had not told yourself. I did not forget the shape of your life. I did not hear a name that built you and file it under practical skills.You forgot my father. You forgot my surname while you were wearing it in every plan we have ever made. Akerlund-Loughrey is not decoration. If his name can fall out of you on a quiet Wednesday in a stone house, I do not know what else of me is optional.I am trying so hard. I need you to hear that without turning it into something you have to fix with a schedule. I am hurt in the specific way that makes my hands go useless. I am hurt in the way that makes me want to shout because if I stay soft you will keep talking like a manual and I will keep pretending the evening is fine.It is not fine.When you disrespect him, something in me closes. I told you all bets are off and I meant it. That is not a threat to leave you on a mountain. That is me telling you there is a line I do not have a clever joke for. He is the line.I do not want a performance of sorry. I do not want you to manage this either. I want you to sound like Scarlett. I want you to know his name the next time it is in the air, immediately, in your body, not after someone has to scream it into a report. I want the woman who sat on that floor with me the first time I said all of this, not the one who issues revisions.If you need it said in one sentence: you went away and left a voice that looks like you, and then that voice forgot my father.That is how much it hurt.Benjamin
